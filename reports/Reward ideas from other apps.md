# Borrow the reveal, skip the casino

The locked reward already has the two hardest pieces that other apps spend years reaching, a pre-commitment and a real object at the end, so the most valuable borrowings are the cheap ones that fill the gaps around them: a head start and a sectioned picture, spare days the user owns, a piece that "arrives" on its own clock after each gym day, a proper win ceremony that leads to a shelf of earned things, and a witness who gets a weekly card instead of one message. All of these run on the phone alone, on a free Apple account, with no points, no money and no server. They work best as four packages built in this order: forgiveness, anticipation, the shelf, then the witness. A home-screen widget and local reminders are the strongest next step and are probably possible without paying, though that needs a device test. Shielding the store's website until the goal is met needs the paid Apple Developer Program plus an entitlement Apple grants on request; duo locks, friend confirmation and reactions need a server and real friends; completion discounts need a brand or affiliate programme. The mechanics to refuse are the ones that made other apps notorious: zero-reset streaks, paid repair, mystery or chance rewards, fake urgency, leagues, pooled money and all-or-nothing team rules. Expect modest gains from each piece: Duolingo's published per-feature lifts are mostly 0.4% to 3.3%, and no study has tested this exact mechanism.

## The feature already has the commitment; it is missing the middle and the day after

Set against the apps in the research, Lock In's locked reward is unusually complete at the two ends. The press-and-hold lock is the same move as Duolingo's streak wager, which produced the largest single lift Duolingo has reported, **+14% Day-7 retention** ([Making Duolingo](https://making.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)), and the product photo is the "picture of the goal" that raised saving in the earmarking field study and that Monzo recommends on its savings pots ([Soman & Cheema](https://www.russellsage.org/sites/default/files/u137/jmr-C-s014-s021-online.pdf), [Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/2018/12/13/locked-pots)). The count is cumulative ("14 of 20"), which sidesteps the best-documented streak harm: seeing a broken streak lowers later engagement, more so when the break is the user's own fault ([CU Boulder on Silverman & Barasch](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)).

What other apps have that the feature lacks sits in three places. The first is the middle of the month, where effort is naturally weakest: café customers bought faster the closer they got to the free coffee, which means the long flat stretch from day 4 to day 15 has no pull of its own ([Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration-illusionary-goal)). The second is a reason to open the app that is not the gym visit itself; Finch and Duolingo both attach a delayed payoff to the day's action so that there is a second, pleasant visit later ([Deconstructor of Fun on Finch](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/x0hd2ssr80y5n7gv0w967pg7hwd7tl)). The third is everything after the checkout opens. Gating a treat behind gym attendance raises visits at first and then fades, notably across a holiday break, in the closest academic analogue to this feature ([Milkman et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25843979/)), so a single lock is a one-month effect unless the win hands the user into the next one. The same study found **61% of participants would pay to have the restriction imposed on them**, which says the appetite for being locked out is real.

One caution frames everything below. A meta-analysis of gamified physical-activity apps found a small short-term effect (Hedges g = 0.32) with feedback the most important element ([JMIR Serious Games](https://games.jmir.org/2025/1/e64410/PDF)), and Duolingo's ex-product chief reports that a mechanic copied from another game without the context that made it work was "completely neutral" after two months of effort ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). The ranking therefore favours ideas that fit what the feature already is over ideas that are famous.

## Twelve phone-only ideas, ranked by pull against effort

The table ranks what can be built today inside the existing app. "Pull" is a judgement from the strength of the evidence and how directly it transfers; none of it was measured on a locked product. "Effort" assumes the current web-view app with its Swift shell.

| Rank | Idea | Borrowed from | Evidence | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Head start: lifting the first tiles "for locking it", so the grid reads 2 of 22, not 0 of 20 | Loyalty cards | 34% vs 19% completion; 10 vs 15 days to finish | Hours |
| 2 | Spare days shown as passes the user owns | Duolingo freeze, emergency reserves | Up to 20% more steps, goal hit on up to 40% more days | Hours (the app already computes spare days) |
| 3 | Picture in sections: rows or quarters that each complete a recognisable part | Battle-pass milestones | Goal gradient; "small regular rewards, bigger milestones" | Hours to a day |
| 4 | Piece arrives later, or the user picks which tile to lift | Finch adventure, Duolingo chest | Retention estimates; uncertainty helps only if resolved fast | A day |
| 5 | Win ceremony distinct from the daily tile flip | Duolingo milestones | +1.7% Day-7 retention for new users | A day |
| 6 | Shelf of earned things with an arrival photo, and the next lock offered there | Peloton shirt, Habitica stable, adiClub level | Inference; fixes the documented fade | One to two days |
| 7 | Short first lock (one to two weeks) and a choice of three targets | Duolingo first week, goal choice | "Significantly increased retention", no figure | Hours |
| 8 | Witness loop: weekly progress card and an outcome message the user must send | stickK referee, STEP UP sponsor | Mixed (see below) | A day |
| 9 | Share grid that shows the month without naming the product | Wordle, Strava stickers | Growth stories only, no conversion data | One to two days |
| 10 | Local reminders: one evening nudge, one "piece ready", one weekly pacing line | Duolingo streak-saver | Duolingo's first big streak win | One to two days in Swift |
| 11 | Price watch on the locked item, plus a declared earmark per gym day | Honey, Klarna, savings pots | Earmarking raised saving; price alerts are an accepted trigger | A day |
| 12 | Missed shelf and a visible kept record, softening the 30-day lockout | Forest stump, stickK record | Inference only | Hours |

The top three are cheap and rest on the firmest research. In the car-wash study a ten-stamp card with two stamps already on it was redeemed by **34% of customers against 19%** for an empty eight-stamp card that needed the same eight washes ([Coglode](https://coglode.com/nuggets/endowed-progress-effect)); the original paper adds that the head start needs a stated reason, so the tiles should lift "for committing", not silently ([Nunes & Drèze](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v32y2006i4p504-512.html)). Slack framed as a reserve, not a lower target, made people persist after a miss, with **up to 20% more steps and the goal hit on up to 40% more days** in a five-week field study, although one small student replication in a diet setting failed ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/), [Utrecht thesis](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/44466)). Lock In already tells the user "you can miss 3 of the 12 days left"; turning that sentence into three visible passes that get spent is a presentation change. Sections follow from the game-design advice to give small regular rewards, bigger milestones and a final reward teased throughout ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2022/6/4/battle-passes-analysis)): four rows of five give four "nearly there" moments instead of one.

The delayed piece is the most distinctive borrowing. In Finch, finishing the day's tasks sends the pet on a six-hour adventure and the evening reward is hearing what it found; in Duolingo a chest earned in the morning opens nine to ten hours later ([Deconstructor of Fun on Finch](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/x0hd2ssr80y5n7gv0w967pg7hwd7tl), [Deconstructor of Fun on Duolingo](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/4/14/duolingo-how-the-15b-app-uses-gaming-principles-to-supercharge-dau-growth)). Finch's estimated Day-1 and Day-7 retention of 54% and 37% edges Duolingo's with no punishment at all, though those are third-party estimates. Because the user already knows the product, the only honest mystery is which part of the picture opens, and uncertain rewards raise effort only when the uncertainty resolves promptly ([Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/how-offering-random-rewards-can-shape-customer-behavior)). So the piece should arrive the same evening, never at month end.

The witness sits lower than its reputation suggests because the evidence is split. stickK's own figures associate a referee and stakes with far higher success, but its users are self-selected ([ICWSM paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/download/31440/33600)). In a randomised step trial, a nominated sponsor who received weekly reports raised activity during the programme but not after it ([Patel et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6735420)); in a class-attendance trial, support alone did worse than nothing while visible comparison raised attendance by about 90% ([Zhang et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5008041/)); and one field experiment found a named supporter halved self-reported weight loss ([UCL blog](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/cbc-digi-hub-blog/?p=1703)). The reading that reconciles these: a witness helps when the user chose someone whose opinion they care about and when that person sees actual progress, not when they are merely told to cheer.

## Four packages that hold together, and the order to build them

Single mechanics matter less than which ones reinforce each other. Four coherent sets fall out of the research, and each is still phone-only.

**Room to miss** combines owned passes, the short first lock, the choice of three targets, and an honest exit when the goal becomes mathematically impossible: the app says so that day and offers to carry the same item into a smaller or later lock. This comes first because expiry pressure is the main burnout trigger game designers name ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2022/6/4/battle-passes-analysis)) and because everything else assumes the user is still there on day 12. The parts need each other: passes without a fixed number chosen at lock time become excuses, and Beeminder's rule of thumb is to allow only the exemptions the user would have written in advance ([Beeminder blog](https://blog.beeminder.com/legit)).

**The picture comes to you** combines the head start, sections, the evening piece, a roughly two-second build-up before each lift, and the "piece ready" reminder. Loot-box research found arousal rises during a short shake before the reveal ([Larche et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-019-09913-5)); Lock In can use that anticipation with no chance involved because the image is known and chosen. This package is what gives the middle of the month its own rhythm, and it is the natural content for a widget later.

**The shelf** combines the win ceremony, a later "has it arrived?" prompt that asks for a photo with the item, a collection of earned things with dates and gym days, a kept record that a miss dents but never zeroes, and the next lock offered from that screen. Duolingo and Peloton keep the milestone moment as pure celebration ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation)), so the next-lock prompt belongs days later, not on the win screen. adidas splits its loyalty points into a spendable balance and a level that never falls ([adidas adiClub](https://www.adidas.com/us/adiclub)); the shelf plays the second role without reintroducing points. It also lets the missed-rewards shelf replace some of the weight of the 30-day lockout, which today delays the user without taking anything from them.

**Someone is watching** combines a prompt to pick a witness "you would be embarrassed to tell", a weekly progress card through the share sheet, the sealed note written by the witness instead of the user, an outcome message that leaves the lock marked "unreported" until it is sent, and the no-spoiler share grid. Sharing a goal raises commitment when the audience is someone whose opinion the user values ([Inverse on Klein et al.](https://inverse.com/innovation/should-you-share-your-goals-heres-what-science-has-to-say)). Wordle's grid spread because it showed how the attempt went without revealing the answer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)), and Strava's sticker drops stats over the user's own photo while leaving out anything they have hidden ([Strava Support](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/17718841-how-do-i-share-my-strava-activity-to-instagram-stories-as-a-transparent-sticker)); a grid of gym days over the still-covered silhouette does both and keeps the price private. This package comes last of the four because its strongest forms need a server.

A fifth, optional tier, **raise the stakes**, suits a minority and should stay opt-in: the user declares where the money is set aside and how much each gym day "earns", or buys the item now and has the witness hold it unopened. When a real self-funded gym commitment contract was offered at work, about one employee in eight took it ([AEA research highlight](https://benny.aeaweb.org/research/how-can-financial-incentives-healthy-habits)), and a study of 1,248 gym members found the people who choose commitment are not reliably the ones with self-control problems ([NBER w26161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26161)). A friend who keeps money on failure is a forfeit by another name; the earlier legal research for this feature found that forfeits are exactly where the gambling question restarts, so the witness should return the item or money either way, only later.

## What has to wait for a paid account, a server, friends or a brand

| Needs | Unlocks | Why it is worth the wait |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device test on the free account (unverified) | Home and lock-screen widget showing the part-uncovered photo and "done today?"; local reminders | Duolingo says half its widget users hold a 6-month streak and rate the widget about as effective as push |
| Paid Apple Developer Program plus Family Controls entitlement | "Shield the shop": the retailer's site and app covered by a screen reading "12 of 20" until the goal is met | Friction with a dismiss option cut app opens 57% in six weeks; no app found doing this for a store |
| Small server and friends with the app | Witness confirms by link, face reactions on progress, duo lock, small-group race | Interdependent rewards beat control by 1,446 steps a day; competition effects outlast support |
| Affiliate or brand agreement | A completion-only discount dropped into the sealed slot, growing with progress | Strava brands pay from $30,000 per challenge for the same "earned purchase" moment |

The widget is the best-evidenced item not yet built. Duolingo's shows only the streak and whether today is done, with art that changes through the day, and installs "skyrocketed" when it was promoted right after a completed lesson ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/widget-feature)). Whether a widget extension and local notifications build cleanly on a free personal team was not covered by the research; from general platform knowledge they should, since neither needs a restricted entitlement, but the app is a web view and a widget is a separate Swift target, so treat this as a few days' work with a real chance of provisioning surprises. Restraint matters more than reach: Duolingo requires executive approval to raise notification volume because opt-outs are permanent ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)).

Shielding the store is the one idea that converts the lock from a promise into friction. Apple's API can cover apps and web domains with a custom screen, but only for sites the user picks in Apple's own picker, and only on that iPhone ([Apple: ShieldSettings](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/managedsettings/shieldsettings), [Apple Developer Forums 708125](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/708125)). The capability does not appear for free personal teams at all ([Apple Developer Forums 764682](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/764682)), and shipping needs a separate distribution entitlement with reported waits from days to over a month ([Apple Developer Forums 809190](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/809190)). The supporting evidence is a six-week study in which a short delay with a dismiss option cut target-app opens by 57%, co-authored by that app's developer, and a later log study showing users click through such friction more often over time ([PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409), [Haliburton et al.](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)). It is a speed bump the user can remove in Settings, and it should be sold as one.

Real social features need both a backend and the first invite, which Duolingo identifies as the biggest drop in its Friend Streak funnel; it caps the feature at five friends and reports a correlational 22% higher daily completion among users who have one ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/)). When that day comes, the trial evidence picks the structure. A reward that depends on the user's own days plus a bonus for each teammate who also finishes beat control by **1,446 steps a day**, while purely individual and purely team rewards did not ([Patel et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4907949)). For Lock In that reads as "your reward opens on your own days; each friend who also makes it adds something", such as an extra pass next month, and never "everyone or no one". GymRats' simplest scoring mode, one point per day with at least one check-in, is already Lock In's unit ([GymRats Help](https://help.gymrats.app/en/articles/10542294-scoring-modes)).

Brand rewards are reachable without Strava's price tag because affiliate tools reverse the money flow: Awin accepts mobile apps as publishers and Shopify Collabs gives a partner a discount code and commission per sale ([Awin](https://success.awin.com/articles/en_US/Knowledge/Can-I-promote-using-mobile-app), [Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/collabs)). The sealed slot is the ready-made container, since a code revealed only on verified completion cannot leak the way coupon-site codes do. Accrue Savings' model, where the merchant adds a reward as the customer progresses toward one product, suggests pitching a discount that grows at the halfway mark and at completion ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/?p=1009559)). Three unknowns remain: per-user single-use codes were not documented for either network, some programmes cancel commission on unauthorised codes, and Apple's rules on affiliate links were not researched. Any commission must be disclosed beside the checkout button. A sponsor paying for the reward, as on Throne, requires the platform to act as merchant so the giver never sees the address ([Elgato Throne page](https://www.elgato.com/ca/en/s/throne)); that is custody and stays out.

## Streak resets, chance and pressure would damage this feature

A consecutive-day streak that resets to zero is the first to refuse. Paceline reset its weekly streak to zero after one missed week ([Paceline support](https://paceline.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/150000216422-streak-overview)), the opposite of the reserve evidence, and the streak researchers advise against framing any message around a break ([University of Delaware](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman)). Paid repair of a missed day is catalogued as a deceptive pattern ([Deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/articles/when-a-user-uses-duolingo-on-a-series-of-consecutive-days-this-is-called-a-streak-if-users-miss-a-day-they-can-pay-to-repair-it)); passes must be granted at lock time and never sold or begged for afterwards. Leagues and public leaderboards need the points currency the user removed and a population the app lacks, and where they exist they cut the most active Fitbit users' steps by about 630 a day while helping the sedentary ([arXiv 2301.02767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02767)).

Chance is the second family. Mystery rewards tied to a purchase, prize draws for finishers and staged near-misses all borrow from gambling: near-misses are unpleasant yet increase the urge to continue ([Clark et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737)), the FTC fined Genshin Impact's developer $20 million over loot boxes and obscured costs ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)), and press coverage of the EU's Digital Fairness Act proposal, adopted by the Commission on 11 November and not yet law, describes bans on false low-stock warnings ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/eu-digital-fairness-act-loot-boxes-dark-patterns)). The win screen is a commercial prompt, so it must never carry a fake countdown, a code that expires in minutes, or a hidden price, and "not now" must be as easy as "buy".

The third family is pressure. Guilt copy, a mascot whose distress escalates and Forest's permanent dead tree are the designs reviewers and researchers single out ([Wikipedia: Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_(application))); copy should point at the next row opening, never at the hoodie being lost. All-or-nothing team rules matched the trial arm that failed to beat control. "Work out to unlock your social apps" is a crowded category with a dozen near-identical 2025 to 2026 entrants, such as [EarnIt](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760624453) and [MonkeyLock](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761743000), and it attaches the stake to the day, not to the reward. Pooled pots and held stakes, the DietBet model ([Livestrong](https://livestrong.com/article/13721416-weight-loss-incentive-apps)), break the rule that the app never holds money. And none of the loss mechanics above, including the witness outcome message and the missed shelf, should attach to the guarded weight goal; body weight is only partly under the user's control, so that goal should keep its guards and gain nothing that adds deadline pressure.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "more enticing" should mean here. The famous retention machines are built to make an abstract number feel valuable; Lock In starts with something the user already wants, so its job is the reverse: keep a real object emotionally present for thirty days and make the path to it forgiving enough to finish. That is why the best ideas are presentational and small, and why the worst are the ones that add invented value such as points, chance or status ladders.

Two measurements will decide what to build after the four packages. The first is the share of users who lock a second reward after the first arrives, which tests whether the shelf turns a one-month effect into cycles. The second is whether witnessed locks finish more often than unwitnessed ones in Lock In's own data, because the published evidence is split and that answer determines whether a server and real friends are worth the cost. If witnessed locks do no better, the paid developer account and the store shield are the better next purchase.
