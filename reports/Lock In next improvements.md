# Earn trust, seed squads, then go native

Lock In v3 already has the right parts; the best improvements make them believable, give the social layer a real stake, and get the app onto classmates' phones inside one dense group. On accuracy, photo calorie apps tested against weighed meals came in about **33% low** ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260804/ai-photobased-calorietracking-tools-underestimate-them-by-33)), so every AI result should be a draft the user confirms in a tap or two, and the number should be labelled a rough estimate. On detail, the calorie bar and the progress page stay on top and the new depth goes one tap down: saved meals, a weekly view, a protein-first mode, tap-through sessions, a personal-record banner, a body-part map and a monthly recap. The pull beyond health is what early Facebook actually sold, attention from people you already know, turned into props, plans to lift together, a squad league and sponsor-funded prizes; no evidence shows that a fitness score works as a dating signal, and a pooled cash pot is the riskiest structure available in Washington. The path to a real app has three steps: a hosted HTTPS page this week (which fixes the live camera), accounts limited to uw.edu addresses, then an Expo build on TestFlight. Three things can stop the plan and need attention first: **UW Recreation's posted policy prohibits photography in its facilities** ([UW Recreation](https://www.washington.edu/ima/uw-recreation-policies/)), Washington's My Health My Data Act applies to an app of any size, and Apple will not pass a photo-sharing build without reporting, blocking and account deletion. None of these recommendations has been tested on Lock In's own users, so judge the first group by streak survival at weeks four and ten, not by installs.

## Photo calorie estimates run a third low, so the AI drafts and the user confirms

The honest description of a single-photo estimate is a rough guide that leans low. NIH researchers photographed 102 weighed meals and ran them through Cal AI, MyFitnessPal, Lose It! and Appediet: all four undercounted, by about 33% overall, and the largest miss was fat, roughly **30 g per meal** ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260804/ai-photobased-calorietracking-tools-underestimate-them-by-33)). That work is a conference abstract, so it is preliminary, and it did not report protein error. General vision models do no better on weighed plates, landing roughly a quarter to a third off on calories ([Coburn et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07048v2)), and trained nutritionists judging photos were off by 41% ([Nutrition5k](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Thames_Nutrition5k_Towards_Automatic_Nutritional_Understanding_of_Generic_Food_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)). The direction matters for a gym audience: someone bulking sees less than they ate, and someone cutting eats more than the bar shows.

Portion size, not food recognition, causes most of the error. With the portion known, per-gram calorie error in the same benchmark fell to **9.5%**, and adding depth data cut calorie error from 26.1% to 18.8% ([Nutrition5k](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Thames_Nutrition5k_Towards_Automatic_Nutritional_Understanding_of_Generic_Food_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)). Context is the other cheap lever: text hints and metadata such as meal time and location lowered error by about 76 kcal on average across eight models ([Coburn et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07048v2)). Lock In should add one portion control (small, medium, large), one optional hint line ("cooked in oil", "two scoops"), and send the time of day with each photo; together these buy more accuracy than a better model. The hint matters most for regional dishes: on a global-cuisine benchmark a leading model named dishes correctly 88% of the time when offered choices and under 22% unprompted ([WorldCuisines](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12705v2)). Later, a server can look recognised foods up in a real database, which is how MacroFactor grounds its numbers ([MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/ai-food-logging/)).

The interface pattern that keeps trust is the same across the leaders: the AI proposes and the user confirms. MyFitnessPal's scan lists matches to accept or replace ([MyFitnessPal](https://blog.myfitnesspal.com/meal-scan/)), MacroFactor gives each ingredient an in-place quantity editor and tells users to review every result ([MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/ai-food-logging/)), and a newer pattern is a single "what should change?" box that re-estimates from the correction ([Nutrola](https://nutrola.app/en/help/fix-result-ai-correction)). The centre camera button stays the single way in; the change comes after the shutter, where Lock In's one-tap gym-or-food switch should extend into the meal itself: two to five named items, tappable portions and one correction box. Reviews punish being wrong without a cheap fix far more than being approximate. In a competitor-run sample of June 2026 App Store reviews, Cal AI averaged **2.42 stars**, with users saying they had to correct most scans, and another app drew complaints because one meal returned different numbers on different scans ([MyNetDiary](https://www.mynetdiary.com/diet-app-scorecard-june-2026.html)). A "same as last time" button that reuses stored numbers answers both speed and consistency. No leading app shows a range or a confidence label, so "about 600 kcal, rough estimate", rounded to the nearest 50, is a cheap way to be more honest than the field. Any confidence label should come from observable features (a mixed dish, a soup or drink, many items), because in a 27,000-query test the models' stated confidence did not track accuracy and only the spread between repeat runs did ([Diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/i-asked-ai-to-count-my-carbs-27000-times)).

Gym check-ins need proportionate proof, because no app fully prevents faking. BeReal's design relies on in-app capture and visible labels, not rejection ([BeReal](https://help.bereal.com/hc/articles/7350386715165)); GymRats is looser and accepts tracker screenshots or synced workouts as proof ([GymRats](https://help.gymrats.app/en/articles/10542253-check-in-verification)) and photos from the library ([GymRats](https://help.gymrats.app/en/articles/10542136-how-to-record-a-check-in)). Forfeit is the warning: by one user's account its AI photo checking was pulled after about a week, and lenient human review then made users trust the rules less ([Beeminder forum](https://forum.beeminder.com/t/agi-delayed-my-recent-experience-with-forfeit/11762)). Lock In should take check-in photos only through its own camera, stamp the time on the server, and label how each check-in was proven: photo plus synced workout, photo only, or workout logged without a photo, a state the small app Rally already displays separately ([Rally](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6762428919)). The AI call that sorts gym from food can also be asked whether the image is a photo of a screen; that is an untested suggestion. A dedicated screen-photo detector scored 98.89% on a small lab set but misfires on glass, reflective surfaces and flickering light, which describes a gym, so it should prompt a retake and never reject ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12178)).

Cost is not the constraint; waiting is. Small vision models list at about $0.10 per million input tokens ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)) and image tokens scale with pixel count ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), so a photo resized to between 512 and 1,000 pixels costs roughly $0.0001 to $0.0005 by this report's arithmetic. Response time is counted in seconds: one fast model takes about 4.8 seconds to begin answering on a public tracker ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-5-flash-lite)), and no measured phone-to-answer timing was found. File the check-in the instant the shutter closes, and let the label and numbers fill in when they arrive.

## The calorie bar stays on top; new detail goes one tap down

Lock In's Food tab already matches the layout the leaders share: MyFitnessPal's 2026 home leads with calories remaining and the macro split, then meals ([MyFitnessPal](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/39985611667341)). The cautionary tale is that same company's April 2026 redesign, which drew complaints about oversized cards, extra taps and a diary hidden behind "View All" ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/04/24/myfitnesspal-new-update-complaints/)). "More detailed" should therefore mean depth under the bar, opened by a tap. The same rule keeps the app readable at a glance: a verdict word first, the number second, the ingredients on tap, in one small vocabulary used everywhere, as Oura does with four labels across every score ([Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057791533)). Teach the bar the same way, by ending a short first run on the user's own targets shown in the real bar; Cal AI's roughly 37 onboarding screens ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/?av=3193&id=1739&vt=1)) suit a paywall, not a check-in app.

Four nutrition additions carry the best evidence. Saved and recent meals come first, because logging frequency predicts success while time spent and detail do not ([Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/324562)), and in one trial a simplified-tracking group logged on **97% of days against 49%** for detailed tracking ([JMIR mHealth and uHealth](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e64139/PDF)). A weekly layer comes second (weekly average against goal, days logged this week), since logging decays fast: one MyFitnessPal study fell from 5.4 logging days a week to 1.4 ([JMIR mHealth and uHealth](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e64139/PDF)). Third is a protein-first mode, which Ladder ships for people who only want to hit a protein number ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/workout-app-ladder-launches-nutrition-tracking-experience)). Fourth is calm colour: a reviewer singles out MacroFactor because an over-target week does not turn the screen red ([Outlift](https://outlift.com/macrofactor-review/)). Fibre and sodium belong behind a tap. Reward "logged something today" with a quiet weekly count, and never attach a streak to hitting a calorie number.

The strongest campus-specific feature is dining-hall logging. A first-year at UConn launched an app that re-presents the dining service's posted menus and nutrition; within about seven weeks it had more than **350 daily users** and the dining service was promoting it ([UConn Today](https://today.uconn.edu/?p=243748)). UW publishes the same kind of data through NetNutrition, with a high-protein filter ([UW HFS](https://hfs.uw.edu/eat/food-and-nutrition)), but the tool advertises no export or API ([UW NetNutrition](https://nutrition.hfs.uw.edu/NetNutrition/1)) and the static station tables carry no protein column ([UW HFS](https://hfs.uw.edu/nutrition/location/289/)). A menu pick replaces a photo guess with published values, so it is also an accuracy fix for the meal students eat most. It needs a conversation with Housing & Food Services before any import.

Keeping calories visible carries a known risk that design can contain. A 2025 review of 38 studies linked diet and fitness app use in young adults with more disordered-eating symptoms ([Flinders University](https://news.flinders.edu.au/blog/2025/02/22/fitness-apps-fuelling-disordered-eating/)), while a randomised trial in 200 low-risk undergraduate women found no effect ([National Center for Health Research](https://www.center4research.org/fitness-tracking-apps-eating-disorders/)); the harm concentrates in people already vulnerable. The mitigations are cheap. Meal numbers stay private and never reach the feed, one switch hides calories everywhere (Oura offers exactly this ([Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/lv/articles/8945980160659))), the app sets no automatic low targets and labels no food good or bad, and protein targets get a sensible cap, since a 2.8 g/kg default drew criticism elsewhere ([WellnessPulse](https://wellnesspulse.com/nutrition/snapcalorie-ai-image-tracker-review/)). Skip any feature for banking calories before a night out: in a study of 611 undergraduates who drink, 20% already ate less beforehand ([Higher Education Center](https://hecaod.osu.edu/associations-between-drinking-related-eating-restriction-behaviors-and-alcohol-outcomes-among-college-students/)).

The progress page already covers most of what the best-loved lifting trackers show, and four gaps remain. Hevy flashes a banner the moment a set beats a previous best and stays silent the first time an exercise is logged ([Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/live-pr/)). A body-part map is now mainstream: Strava announced one for all users in March 2026 ([Endurance.biz](https://endurance.biz/2026/industry-news/strava-introduces-muscle-map-and-heart-rate-tracking-via-airpods-pro-3/)). Hevy's free monthly report appears once on Home and then lives under statistics ([Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/monthly-report/)), whereas Strava drew criticism for putting users' own recap behind a paywall ([T3](https://t3.com/tech/dear-strava-we-have-a-paywall-problem-thats-gone-a-step-too-far)). The fourth is a single next-session suggestion, which Alpha Progression gives for every set ([Alpha Progression](https://alphaprogression.com/en)). The cheapest way to add detail without clutter is tap-through: in Hevy every record and calendar mark opens the workout behind it ([Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/gym-progress/)). Blank charts should state their threshold ("log 3 workouts to see your trend"); only about 2% of 809 apps surveyed give new users a guided first action ([Lazyweb Research](https://design.lazyweb.com/blog/what-brand-new-users-see-on-empty-home-screen.md)).

Effort points and efficiency are invented units, and a score survives only when users can anchor it. Whoop bounds Strain at 0 to 21 with four named bands ([Whoop](https://whoop.com/thelocker/how-does-whoop-strain-work-101)), Apple compares the last 7 days with the last 28 in plain words ([DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/07/apples-training-load-vitals-watchos11.html)), and a 2025 review found that **none of 14 wearable composite scores publishes its formula** ([Doherty et al.](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/teb-2025-0001/html)). Lock In's inputs are simple enough to do better: state the formula in one sentence, name the bands, and say "above your usual" before showing the number. Efficiency (points per hour) is the riskiest figure because it reads as a verdict on long or easy sessions, the kind of judgement a Whoop member rejected when the coach called strength work "more of a side dish than a real workout" ([Whoop community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/the-new-whoop-coach-feels-very-different-and-not-in-a-good-way/15960)); rename it intensity and move it one tap down. An AI summary, if added, should be weekly, optional and short, since per-activity recaps that restate visible numbers earned Strava's assistant the nickname "Captain Obvious" ([Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/who-gets-to-interpret-the-workout-user-tensions-with-ai-generated-fitness-feedback)). Session tags (easy day, sick, exam week) give any summary the context it otherwise lacks.

## The pull beyond health is being seen, a squad league and sponsored prizes

What Facebook had at Harvard was not dating as such. Sign-up required a harvard.edu address, 650 people registered on day one and about 4,300 within two weeks ([The Harvard Crimson](https://thecrimson.com/article/2014/2/4/facebook-ten-years-feature-1)), and the main activity was looking up people users already knew: **1.5 million profile views in under a month** ([Harvard Magazine](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/technology/harvard-facebook-history-mark-zuckerberg-winklevoss)). Tinder's first growth ran on the same engine, fraternity members opening the app to find sorority members they recognised ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/253165)). The transferable hook is attention from specific, known people inside a gated community. For Lock In that means uw.edu-only sign-up, a "noticed you" strip (who gave props, who nudged, whose streak is about to lapse), and props that are positive-only and tied to effort, such as showing up at 6 a.m. or reaching week ten.

The romantic angle has soft support as a venue and none as a score. One in five Gen Z respondents in Strava's 2024 survey had dated someone met through a fitness group ([DatingNews](https://www.datingnews.com/industry-trends/running-clubs-why-modern-daters-are-swapping-apps-for-laps/)), and dating apps are moving toward in-person activity themselves: Tinder's events pilot drew 71% of eligible 18-to-24-year-olds in Los Angeles ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/14/gen-z-dating-apps-swiping-irl/)). No source shows that a streak or badge improves anyone's romantic prospects. Where fitness apps drifted toward dating mechanics the result was discomfort: women in one running group reacted badly to Strava's direct messages ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-direct-messaging/)), and stalking through fitness apps is documented ([The Telegraph via Yahoo](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/strava-stalkers-forced-fitness-apps-071000604.html)). The safe translation is co-attendance among people who already know each other: "lifting at 5, who's in?" plans, squads that meet in person, and launch events run through chapters and clubs. Features that surface strangers by gym schedule, show non-friends who is at the IMA, make check-in photos browsable, or rank people by photo reactions (which ranks appearance) would recreate those harms and are not recommended.

Competition has the strongest experimental support of any social frame. In an 11-week trial with nearly 800 Penn students, the competition arms attended about **90% more** than controls, while teams built only on mutual support attended less than people with no social feature at all ([Annenberg School](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/news-events/news/want-exercise-more-get-yourself-some-competition)); in a six-month workplace trial only the competition arm kept its gain after the programme ended ([Penn Medicine](https://www.pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2019/september/using-a-wearable-device-to-exercise-more-add-competition-to-improve-your-results)). That is a caution for Lock In's shared squad streak, which resembles the support-only condition. Add a quarter-long league that ranks squads against each other with weekly standings, scored on check-ins per member so large groups do not win on size; UW's staff Winter Games averaged participant scores for the same reason ([The Whole U](https://thewholeu.uw.edu/2026/03/05/huskies-show-up-strong-winter-games-highlights-and-winners/)). Count showing up, never performance or looks, and put squads on any campus-wide board, not named individuals. School against school can wait for a second dense campus; the Pac-12 Fitness Challenge worked as a one-week event ([Athletic Business](https://www.athleticbusiness.com/operations/programming/article/15146939/how-interactive-competitions-are-driving-fitness-participation)).

Money works as a launch and re-activation tool, not as the retention engine. Students paid to attend a gym roughly doubled their visits afterwards (0.60 to 1.24 a week at Chicago), with nearly all the gain among those who rarely went ([Charness & Gneezy](https://faculty.econ.ucsb.edu/~charness/papers/exercise.pdf)), and the best of 53 programmes in a 60,000-member gym study paid about nine cents for returning after a missed workout ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits)). Yet $30 to $60 incentives left no lasting change among 836 new gym members ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w23567)), and only about one in eight people accept a deposit contract ([American Economic Association](https://swlb1.aeaweb.org/research/how-can-financial-incentives-healthy-habits)). The law narrows the options further. Washington treats charging a fee to join a contest of chance as professional gambling ([Washington State Gambling Commission](https://wsgc.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/Illegal%20Gambling%20Activities.pdf)) and counts a game as chance whenever chance plays a material role ([Perkins Coie](https://www.perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/no-game-changer-for-fantasy-sports-in-washington.html)); Apple calls contests among the most regulated offerings on its store ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)); and the FTC settled with Pact, a gym-stakes app, for $1.5 million over wrongful charges ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017/09/22/fitness-app-company-settles-ftc-nearly-1-million/)). What Washington does allow is a promotional contest that costs nothing to enter ([RCW 9.46.0356](https://law.justia.com/codes/washington/2018/title-9/chapter-9.46/section-9.46.0356/index.html)). The workable stack is a comeback bonus, a milestone perk from a U-District business, a free-entry quarterly draw funded by a sponsor, and a donation to the winning squad's cause. The last fits UW: its 4,000-member Greek community gave **$250,000 in 2025** ([UW Office of Fraternity & Sorority Life](https://www.washington.edu/ofsl/)), and the staff Winter Games drew 1,800 participants with BECU and Husky Mobile as sponsors ([The Whole U](https://thewholeu.uw.edu/2026/03/05/huskies-show-up-strong-winter-games-highlights-and-winners/)). A self-set penalty in the Forfeit style is the only cash stake worth considering later, and only after a lawyer has reviewed it.

Launch into one pre-connected group, not into "UW". The campus apps that worked reached a visible share of a small community fast: about 40% of one school in a day for tbh ([recall.it](https://www.recall.it/summary/entrepreneurship/how-to-consistently-go-viral-nikita-biers-playbook-for-winning-at-consumer-apps)) and more than half of one in three hours for Saturn ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer)). UW Recreation alone counts **28,824 student users** ([UW Recreation](https://depts.washington.edu/safcom/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Recreation-FY26-Budget-Request-Packet.pdf)), so the comparable unit is a fraternity or sorority chapter, a Rec Club or a residence-hall floor, seeded at one synchronised moment with a named student lead and a small reward for showing the installed app. Pay buys installs, not habit: a BeReal ambassador at Brown earning $30 per referral signed up about ten students and quit within a month because few people posted ([The Brown Daily Herald](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal)). Campus rules bite as well. Fizz's door-to-door launch at Lafayette drew threats of discipline ([The Lafayette](https://lafayettestudentnews.com/141938/news/fizz-floods-campus-defying-regulations/)), and UW Recreation bars commercial gain and unapproved flyers in its buildings ([UW Recreation](https://www.washington.edu/ima/uw-recreation-policies/)), so work through registered student groups.

Plan for decay from the start. Gas fell from 3.1 million downloads in its peak month to 2,900 ten months later ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago)), Lapse's forced-invite wall preceded a 70% drop in downloads ([Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20231117/5-lapse-loses-momentum-as-downloads-drop-70-percent)), and Fizz must replace about a quarter of its users every year as they graduate ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/marketers/new-social-media-platform-fizz-gives-brands-a-crash-course-in-marketing-to-college-students/)). Saturn's founders credit their mid-30s percent day-30 retention to a utility that works for one person while the network fills in ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer)); Lock In's calorie bar and progress page play that role, which is the evidence for keeping them. Invites should be the duo and the squad themselves, each of which needs a named friend, so growth does not depend on a Share button. Re-seed every September at Dawg Daze, where UW Recreation's fair hosts more than 40 Rec Clubs ([UW Recreation](https://www.washington.edu/ima/dawg-daze-2026-at-uw-rec/)). Keep shared streaks blameless too: 86% of young Snapchat users maintain streaks, yet 42% would remove the feature if they could ([Bits of Freedom](https://www.bitsoffreedom.nl/en/2026/03/10/the-influence-of-snapchats-gamification-features-on-young-people/)), so let either partner pause a duo without a visible break, never charge to restore, and word nudges as invitations.

## A hosted page this week, uw.edu accounts next, then Expo on TestFlight

The first step costs nothing and fixes the live camera. Browsers grant camera access only to secure pages, and only to a top-level document unless the embedding page allows it ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia)), which matches how the prototype's camera fails inside its current frame. Served from its own HTTPS address on a static host such as Cloudflare Pages, whose free tier includes server functions ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/functions/pricing/)), the same file works in Safari. Two things must change at that point. The AI call has to go through a small server function that holds the API key ([Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9767949-api-key-best-practices-keeping-your-keys-safe-and-secure)), with a spend cap and an invite code so the endpoint is not an open wallet, and photos should be resized before upload. Added to the Home Screen, the page can send push notifications on iPhone without an Apple developer account ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). The ceiling is firm, though: no install prompt, no background sync, and no web route to widgets or Apple Health ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). Browser storage can also be evicted ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)), so a streak stored only on the device can vanish.

Real friends need accounts and a database. Supabase fits most directly: 50,000 monthly users free, row-level security for friends-only visibility, and a documented hook that rejects sign-ups from outside an allowed email domain ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing), [Supabase docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/before-user-created-hook)). Its free projects pause after a week idle, so the $25 plan is the practical floor once classmates depend on it. Photo bytes drive the bill at every scale, and storing them in Cloudflare R2, which charges nothing for egress ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)), flattens it. By this report's arithmetic from list prices, 1,000 active students cost roughly **$25 to $35 a month for the backend and $9 to $45 for vision calls**.

Widgets, a Lock Screen countdown and Apple Health require a native build, and Expo is the best fit for a solo developer who already works in JavaScript. Its current SDK provides a live camera view ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/camera/)), a free push service ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/faq/)) and a widgets and Live Activities library that is still young ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/widgets/)); HealthKit comes through a community package ([GitHub](https://github.com/kingstinct/react-native-healthkit)). The price is rewriting the interface in React Native. Swift reaches every Apple API directly but forfeits Android, and wrapping the web page risks Apple's rule against repackaged websites ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). iPhone-first is safe for this audience, since 88% of US teens own one ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/04/09/teen-iphone-ownership-continues-to-soar/)). The closing-window reminder and Lock Screen countdown are the native features that matter most: BeReal users with accounts often simply forgot to post ([The Brown Daily Herald](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal)), and Locket's weekly Lock Screen prompt is answered by over 25% of its active users ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/03/lockets-social-app-is-picking-up-steam-with-gen-alpha/)). TestFlight covers a campus pilot with up to **10,000 external testers** through a public link ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/testflight/)) at $99 a year ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/fee-waivers/)), and its builds must already meet the review guidelines. One solo developer's Expo and Supabase app took about three months of evenings and weekends ([DEV](https://dev.to/dusttoo/i-built-and-launched-a-mobile-app-in-3-months-as-a-solo-engineer-heres-exactly-what-happened-479e)); the hosted page takes days.

The simulated connections become real unevenly. Apple Health needs only store review and, since iOS 26, lets an app run a workout session on the phone alone ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/healthkit.md)); Health Connect follows when Android ships. Strava cannot feed a friends feed, because its API agreement allows a user's data to be shown only to that user ([Strava](https://www.strava.com/legal/api)), and Garmin has paused new developer applications ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/14/garmin-developer-api-access-paused/)); workouts from both still arrive when users sync them into Apple Health. Background sync is unreliable enough that GymRats tells users to open the app after a workout ([GymRats](https://help.gymrats.app/en/articles/13182186-about-auto-sync)), so a streak deadline must never depend on silent sync. For moderation, automated image checks cost about a tenth of a cent each ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/rekognition/pricing/)).

## Nine blockers, three to clear before the first classmate signs up

The first three rows decide whether a pilot can run at all. Nothing here is legal advice; a short consult with a student legal clinic before any public launch is proportionate.

| Blocker | What the sources say | Way through |
|---|---|---|
| **Gym photo rules** (clear first) | UW Recreation prohibits photography and video in its facilities without prior approval and carves out no exception for selfies ([UW Recreation](https://www.washington.edu/ima/uw-recreation-policies/)); Life Time, Equinox and Crunch bar filming other members ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/03/gym-influencer-film-workout-tiktok)). Whether UW enforces the rule against phone selfies is unknown. | Ask UW Recreation for approval now. Until then, offer proof that needs no interior photo (an entrance or outside shot, or a synced workout) and remind users at capture to keep other people and locker rooms out of frame. |
| **Washington health-data law** (clear first) | The My Health My Data Act has no size threshold, covers measurements and data inferred by algorithms, and requires opt-in consent separate from general terms ([RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010), [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/my-health-my-data-washingtons-health-data-privacy-revolution.md)); a private class action has already been filed under it ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250220-first-lawsuit-filed-under-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act)). Whether a solo, unincorporated developer counts as a "legal entity" was not answered. | A separate health-data privacy policy, one opt-in to collect and another to share with friends, working delete, no ad or analytics SDKs that receive health events, no face recognition. |
| **App Store rules** (clear first) | Apps with user content need filtering, reporting, blocking and a published contact; apps with accounts need in-app deletion; health apps must disclose the method behind accuracy claims; sending personal data to a third-party AI needs explicit permission ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). TestFlight builds are held to the same rules. | Build the compliance set before the external beta, label calorie figures as estimates, and add a consent line for AI processing. |
| **Cash stakes** | Paid-entry contests of chance are gambling in Washington ([Washington State Gambling Commission](https://wsgc.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/Illegal%20Gambling%20Activities.pdf)); Google Play bars paid-entry prize contests outside licensed categories ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)). Money-transmitter rules were not researched. | No pooled pot. Free-entry, sponsor-funded draws with official rules shown in the app, and legal review before any money feature ships. |
| **Campus solicitation rules** | UW Recreation bars commercial gain and unapproved flyers in the IMA ([UW Recreation](https://www.washington.edu/ima/uw-recreation-policies/)). | Launch through registered student organisations and seek a UW Recreation partnership. |
| **Calorie numbers and disordered eating** | Psychologists warn that low calorie targets and gamified goals can produce shame ([AP via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/07/01/nutrition-apps-can-help-build-healthy-habits-for-some-users-their-gaming-features-carry-risks)). | Private numbers, a hide-calories switch, no red, no calorie streaks and no automatic targets. |
| **Third-party data terms** | Strava's display rule ([Strava](https://www.strava.com/legal/api)); UW dining data has no advertised API ([UW NetNutrition](https://nutrition.hfs.uw.edu/NetNutrition/1)). | Read workouts through Apple Health only, and ask Housing & Food Services before importing menus. |
| **Age rules** | Texas's app-store age law is in force and California's starts on 1 January 2027 ([Ashurst Perkins Coie](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/supreme-court-green-lights-the-texas-age-verification-law-for-app-stores/), [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/an-app-developers-guide-to-app-store-age-assurance-laws/)). A few first-years will be 17. | An honest age rating and an 18+ sign-up gate for the pilot; integrate the stores' age signals before a public release. |
| **Health breach rule** | The FTC's rule reaches apps that draw identifiable health data from more than one source and requires notice to users within 60 days of a breach ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0)). | Collect the minimum, keep access logs, and treat any unconsented disclosure as a breach. |

## Conclusion

The two rounds of research now point the same way as the owner's preferences. The calorie bar and the progress page are the part of Lock In that works for one person, and that is what kept the surviving campus apps alive while their networks filled in; the social layer's job shifts from sharing outward to being noticed, lifting together and competing in small groups. Trust is the common thread across all four areas, and it has three levels: numbers that admit they are estimates, check-ins that say how they were proven, and rules the app states only if it enforces them.

The most valuable work this month is not code. Two conversations, with UW Recreation about check-in photos and with Housing & Food Services about menu data, decide whether the core mechanic is allowed where most students train and whether the best campus feature can exist. UW Recreation is both the gatekeeper and the highest-reach partner on campus. Three unknowns remain open and should be measured in the first seeded group: whether a 48-hour window retains better than a daily one (no experiment exists), what share of AI meal drafts users correct, and how many check-ins rely on each kind of proof.
