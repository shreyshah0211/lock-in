# Shrink Lock In to one loop

Lock In gets better by removing things. The evidence supports one tiny action (a live gym photo), a forgiving 48-hour streak, a friends feed with one-tap reactions and one small-group construct; the tracking suite (nutrition, water, lift logs, AI plan), the money pot and the stories row are separate products that compete with the check-in for effort. The stakes are real: a typical health-and-fitness app keeps only **3% to 8% of installers at day 30** ([Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks)), and BeReal, the nearest single-mechanic precedent, lost **48% to 61% of its daily users within about six months** of its peak ([Platformer](https://platformer.news/how-bereal-missed-its-moment), [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/02/22/bereal-may-be-on-the-out-users-have-nearly-halved-since-peak)). "Modern" in 2026 is structural, not decorative: edge-to-edge content, a small floating tab bar with three tabs, one oversized status number and one labelled action in the thumb zone. On integrations, Apple Health is the only connection that matters at launch and Health Connect is second; Strava's terms forbid showing one user's data to another, Garmin is closed to new developers, Fitbit sits behind a restricted Google review and MyFitnessPal has no open API, so a connections screen should offer the two hubs, not a row per brand. GymRats, the closest comparable, reports **more than 4 million registered users** on little more than photo check-ins inside friend challenges ([GymRats](https://partners.gymrats.app)), the best sign that the core is enough. Three additions carry stronger evidence than most of the original feature set: a first run that reaches the camera in under five screens, streak repair after a miss, and off-app surfaces (a widget, a Lock Screen countdown, a recap).

## Modern in 2026 means floating controls, one giant number and three tabs

Both platforms converged in 2025 on thin floating controls over edge-to-edge content, then split on surface. iOS 26 brought Liquid Glass and a tab bar that floats over content and shrinks on scroll ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). Google's Material 3 Expressive instead makes the key action bigger, lower and more colourful: across 46 studies and more than 18,000 participants, people found key elements **up to 4x faster**, and preference peaked at **up to 87% among 18 to 24 year olds**, though stripping text labels or scrambling layouts for style lowered usability ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Copying Liquid Glass literally is the riskier path. Nielsen Norman Group faulted iOS 26 for illegible text over photos, shrunken touch targets and decorative motion ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)), a beta five weeks after launch added a higher-contrast "Tinted" setting ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-beta-4-adds-new-setting-to-tone-down-liquid-glass-transparency/)), and Apple's own guidance keeps glass out of the content layer and suggests a **35% dark dimming layer** behind clear controls that float on bright media ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). For a photo-first app with a college audience the recipe is a floating pill tab bar, opaque cards and numbers, a dimmed strip under any text on a gym photo, and one bold, labelled action.

The best-loved apps earn "simple" by cutting tabs and leading with one to three values. Oura collapsed **five tabs into three**, and its October 2025 refresh opens on a single daily insight ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/new-oura-app-experience/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/20/oura-app-redesign/)). WHOOP's home shows three dials, the main score at roughly 72pt, with trends one tap down ([WHOOP](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/the-all-new-whoop-home-screen/), [925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown)). Gentler Streak cut the suggestions on its first tab to one recommendation ([iThinkDifferent](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/?p=335919)), and Cal AI shows calories and macros while hiding everything else behind a swipe ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/cal-ai-calorie-tracker)). StepsApp pairs a floating tab bar with a separate action button ([StepsApp](https://steps.app/blog/tech/new-stepsapp-for-ios-26)), which fits Apple's rule that tab bars navigate and do not hold actions ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). The social apps go further and delete decisions. Original BeReal had no filters, no camera-roll uploads, hidden friend counts and a feed that unlocked only after posting ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02883v1)). Retro gives each friend exactly one card a week ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/07/retro-is-a-deeply-personal-photo-journaling-app-for-close-friends/)). Locket puts friends' photos in a home-screen widget, and its weekly prompt arrives as a Lock Screen Live Activity that **over 25% of active users** answer ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/03/lockets-social-app-is-picking-up-steam-with-gen-alpha/)). The counter-case is BeReal after its sale: video, chat, two discovery feeds and ads arrived while downloads fell roughly 50% in a year ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/07/16/bereal-comeback-strategy)).

Onboarding splits into two models, and Lock In belongs in the short one. Cal AI runs 28 quiz steps and Bevel 34 because a paywall waits at the end, and Cal AI's founder says extra questions raised conversion ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/cal-ai-calorie-tracker), [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/bevel-health-performance), [Founderpath](https://founderpath.com/resources/zach-yadegari-cal-ai-onboarding-tests-and-influencer-marketing)). That is a pricing device. Ladder takes four steps to a named "Welcome Workout" ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/ladder-strength-training-plans)), Duolingo asks seven questions and defers the account ([UserGuiding](https://userguiding.com/blog/duolingo-onboarding-ux)), and pushing Duolingo's sign-up wall later lifted daily users **about 20%** ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)). Apple wants onboarding taught through the real action, each permission requested when its feature is first used, and any priming screen limited to a single "Continue" button ([Apple HIG, onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding), [Apple HIG, privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)). BeReal's first run shows what to avoid: a contacts request before any value, then a "friends haven't posted" screen for someone with no friends ([Tear Them Down](https://tearthemdown.substack.com/p/bereal-product-case-study)). For Lock In that means three to five screens, then the camera, with the first check-in doubling as the tutorial.

What reads as dated is consistent across critics: greetings and referral blocks in prime space and a most-used control that is too small or unlabeled ([IXD@Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/02/design-critique-crunch-fitness-ios-app/)), plus dashboards of equally weighted cards with no clear next step ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/ladder-strength-training-plans)). In low-star reviews of the top 20 US health and fitness apps, billing (45% of apps), paywalls (40%) and ads (30%) outrank layout complaints ([Unstar](https://unstar.app/hi/research)), so any upsell on Home will read as clutter first. One limit: no source documents how Strava, Hevy, Duolingo, WHOOP or Oura adopted the 2025 design languages; the verified adopters are mostly indie apps.

## Apple Health covers most of the value; most other doors are closed

The audience decides the order. **87% to 88% of US teens own an iPhone** ([Apple World Today](https://appleworld.today/2025/10/survey-eighty-seven-percent-of-teens-report-they-own-an-iphone/), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/04/09/teen-iphone-ownership-continues-to-soar/)), while the most recent Apple Watch figure located for teens is 34% in spring 2022 ([Apple World Today](https://appleworld.today/study-shows-that-u-s-teens-really-like-the-iphone-airpods-apple-watch-apple-pay)). No current figure exists, but most students probably wear no watch, so the photo stays the default and device data is an enhancement. HealthKit and Health Connect need only store review, and since iOS 26 a third-party app can run a workout session on the iPhone alone ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/healthkit.md)). Two limits matter. Sync is imperfect: GymRats tells users to open the app after a workout because Apple Health data might not arrive while the app is closed ([GymRats](https://help.gymrats.app/en/articles/13182186-about-auto-sync)), so a streak deadline cannot depend on silent background sync. And App Review bars using health data for advertising ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

Aggregators do not change this picture. Terra starts at $399 to $499 a month and its own price list works out to about $1 per extra connected source per month ([Terra](https://tryterra.co/pricing)); Junction charges $300 a month with no startup discount ([Junction](https://support.junction.com/articles/8030815274-startup-partnership-and-custom-pricing-options)). That is steep for a free student app. Bevel shows the alternative works: it is built on Apple Health as its wearable hub and reports more than 100,000 daily users (company-reported) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/30/bevel-raises-10m-series-a-from-general-catalyst-for-its-ai-health-companion)).

Leading apps present integrations the same way: one settings screen with a row and toggle per source, automatic import, a visible origin label on each imported workout and silent de-duplication ([WHOOP](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/how-whoop-and-strava-work-together/), [GymRats](https://help.gymrats.app/en/articles/13182186-about-auto-sync)). GymRats also accepts a synced device workout as verification in place of a photo, with optional location ([GymRats](https://help.gymrats.app/en/articles/10542253-check-in-verification)). Lock In should borrow the badge and not the substitution, because the photo is the feed: a device-only check-in puts a stats card where a friend's photo should be. A detected workout works better as a prompt to add the photo. No app was found doing exactly that, so the design is an inference to test. Location stays optional: Apple forbids requiring it, and background gym detection needs the "Always" permission ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [AFCore](https://afcore-docs.pages.dev/entitlements/gym-visits)).

The connections that drive growth are not health APIs. Instagram Stories accepts a background image plus a sticker from any app with a Facebook App ID ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/sharing-to-stories)), and Snapchat's Creative Kit adds an attachment URL that works as an invite link ([Snap](https://developers.snap.com/snap-kit/creative-kit/overview)). Apple recommends Live Activities for workout sessions, and Strava already shows live stats on the Lock Screen ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322), [Strava](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401559-strava-live-activities-on-ios)). No data was found on how often Stories cards convert to installs.

## Forgiving streaks and small groups retain; strictness and add-ons do not

Streaks are the best-documented retention mechanic, and the wins come from leniency. At Duolingo, letting one lesson extend the streak raised day-14 retention **3.3%** and the number of learners on a streak of seven days or more by over 40% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Two streak freezes beat one ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/)), three added nothing, and a free "earn back" after a loss won without cheapening the streak ([recall.it, secondary](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)). These are company-reported lifts at huge scale. Independent work agrees on direction. In a 61,293-member gym megastudy, the top programme of 54 paid small rewards for returning after a missed workout ([Milkman et al.](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/mys88-cc756)). A four-year study of more than 30,000 gym members found a gym habit takes **about six months** to form, with long gaps between visits predicting failure ([Caltech](https://neuroscience.caltech.edu/news/no-magic-number-for-time-it-takes-to-form-habits)). And displaying a broken streak depresses later engagement, less so when the streak can be repaired ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). Lock In's 48-hour window is already a flexibility rule that fits how people train, and a two-pass cap matches Duolingo's result. Repair after a miss is the best-evidenced addition, kept limited so slack does not become the norm. No public experiment compares a 48-hour window with a daily one, so that advantage is inferred.

A streak number alone will not carry six months, so the social layer has to hold retention. The proven pieces are cheap acknowledgment and small bounded groups. Strava logged **14 billion kudos** in 2025 and reached 1 million clubs ([Strava](https://www.webull.com/news/13961203642123264)). Duolingo users with a Friend Streak are 22% more likely to finish their daily lesson, the feature is capped at five friends, and the first invite is the bottleneck ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/)). Hevy reached 2 million downloads by 2023 on roughly $15,000 of ads through word of mouth and a follow graph ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/guillem-ros-hevy-podcast)). Leaderboards cut both ways: they raised learning time 17% at Duolingo ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)), yet in a lab study with undergraduates top ranks grew complacent and bottom ranks' extra effort faded after repeated discouraging feedback ([Na & Han](https://www.emerald.com/intr/article/33/7/1/178330)). Keep them inside friend groups. Strava's Gen Z members are twice as likely as Gen X to call weight training their main sport and lean toward fitness as a way to meet people ([Strava](https://www.webull.com/news/13961203642123264)), so the hook beyond "being healthy" is social obligation, not money. Stakes work for self-selected adults (73% success in StepBet games), but the operator has since spun those apps off ([WayBetter](https://waybetter.app/science), [WayBetter](https://support.waybetter.app/hc/en-us/articles/19582162879251-Were-the-StepBet-DietBet-apps-sold-and-what-does-that-mean-for-my-account)), and the legality of cash pots for US students was not researched.

Viral growth for this age group comes from density, invites that are part of the product, and outputs made for other apps. Fizz is on more than 620 campuses ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/03/college-social-app-fizz-expands-into-grocery-delivery)), and BeReal's first wave came from campuses and paid student ambassadors ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal)). Locket hit 2 million sign-ups in 11 days with a five-friend cap and a product that is useless alone ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/locket-an-app-for-sharing-photos-to-friends-homescreens-hits-the-top-of-the-app-store)), whereas Duolingo's rewarded referral programme added only 3% new users ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). Spotify Wrapped drew about 500 million shares in 2025 ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-wrapped-campaign-hit-200m-engaged-users-in-24-hours-a-19-yoy-increase)). The same density speeds decay: Gas reached 1 million daily users in two months and was shut down fifteen months after launch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago)).

The harms are conditional and each has a design answer. Among 105 people diagnosed with an eating disorder, 73% of the MyFitnessPal users said the app contributed ([Duke Psychiatry](https://psychiatry.duke.edu/blog/trouble-tracking)), although a randomized trial in low-risk undergraduate women found no effect ([NCHR](https://www.center4research.org/fitness-tracking-apps-eating-disorders/)). Appearance-focused fitness images raised body dissatisfaction in women aged 17 to 25 ([Flinders University](https://news.flinders.edu.au/blog/2020/03/06/women-deflated-by-fitspo-images/)), and Strava users who seek praise trend toward obsessive use while those who give encouragement do not ([Cycling Weekly](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/news/latest-news/danger-that-strava-can-fuel-obsessive-tendencies-according-to-new-study-448096)). An "I showed up" photo with reactions is the low-risk artifact; calorie numbers on a social app for college students are the high-risk one.

## Keep eight features and park the tracking suite

The prototype as last built on 16 November 2026 (five tabs, with a stories row, gym busyness meter, money pot and water tracking beside the feed, challenges, nutrition and grid) carried roughly a dozen distinct user actions where successful launches started with one or two. Its working source was already being simplified along these lines while this report was written, so some rows below are likely done. Ladder added nutrition only after passing 300,000 paid members ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251027087979/en)), BlaBlaCar removed more than 50 features during 2017 while still shipping new experiences ([BlaBlaCar](https://medium.com/blablacar-design/what-we-learned-from-simplifying-our-product-in-2017-9087d16869f)), and a mechanic Duolingo borrowed from a puzzle game cost two months and moved nothing ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). The readings below are judgments from the cited evidence; none has been tested on Lock In's users.

| Decision | Features | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Keep** | Photo check-in with 48-hour streak; rest passes (cap two); one closing-window reminder and friend nudges; one-photo-per-friend feed with reactions; duo streaks; one group construct with an in-group leaderboard; activity grid on the profile; share card | Direct or close-analogue evidence for retention or growth |
| **Reduce** | Post-photo form (type, minutes, effort, audience, lifts); comments and squad chat; tiers, badges, effort points and efficiency (overlapping scores); profile stats tiles; per-brand connection rows; campus rivalry and campus leaderboard | Each adds input or reading cost; rivalry needs density on two campuses first |
| **Cut from v1** | Nutrition and water tracking; lift logging and charts; AI weekly plan; money pot; stories row; gym busyness meter; Meal and Progress camera modes; photo-library uploads | Weak tie to the loop, a separate product (Hevy, Cal AI), no evidence found, or real risk (calorie data, cash) |

Nutrition deserves a direct word because it is the feature furthest from the check-in and the one with documented risk. If it stays, the evidence points to Cal AI's shape: camera-first logging, one calories-and-macros summary, opt-in, private, and off the tab bar. Progress photos, if they return later, belong private by default.

## Conclusion: fifteen changes, in priority order

The research inverts the usual prototype instinct. The most-built parts of the first build (food, lifts, AI plan) have the weakest evidence, while the best-supported ones (repair after a miss, a first run that ends in the camera, a presence on the home and lock screens) were absent from it. The integration market also closed sharply between 2024 and 2026, which helps a newcomer: Apple Health hands a startup the same workout data incumbents get, and nobody new gets Strava or Garmin directly. Three unknowns should be tested, not assumed: whether gating the feed until check-in helps at a 48-hour cadence, whether Stories cards convert to installs, and how many students actually wear a watch. Measure success by window, not by day ("checked in within each 48-hour window" and weekly retention), against category medians of roughly 27% at day 1 and 3% to 8% at day 30 ([Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/what-makes-a-good-retention-rate/), [Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks)). A University of Washington launch also falls under Washington's My Health My Data Act, which requires a separate health-privacy policy and unbundled opt-in consent and carries a private right of action ([Washington State Bar News](https://wabarnews.org/2024/04/09/the-washington-my-health-my-data-act/)), so every data type cut is also an obligation removed.
